BACKGROUND / COVER STORY

Subject is the man behind the only literal headline in the public record that uses the phrase at the center of this file: "McDaniel wins Gulf seat" (The Star, Port St. Joe, twenty ten). No cover needed: the file is twelve years of county meeting minutes, federal filings, and election returns, all public.

The seat is District 2 of the Gulf County Board of County Commissioners — the county on Florida's northern Gulf coast between Panama City and Apalachicola, county seat Port St. Joe.

SERVICE RECORD (CAREER FILE)

  • 2010 — The Gulf seat. Incumbent Billy Traylor was knocked out by Tom Semmes in the Republican primary. Subject then defeated Semmes in the general election, sixty-two percent to thirty-eight — six hundred thirty-four votes to three hundred ninety-one. Subject's own framing, on the record: "Our (McDaniel and Semmes) goal was to clean up District 2, and that's what happened." Semmes, conceding: "Ward is a good guy, and he will do a good job."
  • 2011 — In office. Appears as Commissioner, District 2, in board minutes from February twenty eleven. Early entry of note: he pressed the county to protect the pay of employee-volunteers serving on the search and rescue unit, after five people lost their lives on the Apalachicola River in twenty ten.
  • 2014–2015 — Chairman. Chairman of the board by March twenty fourteen. Federal filings address him by title: a May twenty fourteen FEMA flood-hazard notice in the Federal Register is addressed to "The Honorable Ward McDaniel, Chairman, Gulf County Board of Commissioners." State financial filings for the Dead Lakes Water Management District (fiscal twenty thirteen through twenty fifteen) likewise name "T. Ward McDaniel" as the chairman reporting.
  • 2016–2017 — Chairman again. Reappointed Chairman for twenty seventeen (motion carried November twenty-two, twenty sixteen). The same meeting appointed him to the Small County Coalition board for twenty seventeen, alongside Commissioner Sandy Quinn; he also appears as a Gulf County representative to the coalition for fiscal twenty sixteen–seventeen.
  • 2017 — Hurricane Irma. Chaired the board's emergency sessions of September twenty seventeen as Irma approached — sand staged for Indian Pass residents, the District 1 seat sitting vacant beside him awaiting a governor's appointment.
  • 2019–2022 — Senior commissioner. Continuous service in the minutes through the pandemic years, one of the board's senior members, moving routine county business (ordinances, budget amendments, beach-driving permit rules).
  • October 25, 2022 — The recognition. The board formally honored him: Chairman Quinn had County Attorney Novak read a proclamation acknowledging his years of service to the citizens of Gulf County, and the members presented him with a plaque. The motion carried four to zero, subject abstaining. It reads, in the record, as a farewell.
  • 2022 — The seat changes hands (addendum, resolved 2026-11-09). Billy Traylor — the same incumbent subject's 2010 run had displaced — beat subject in the 2022 Republican primary, then won the November 8, 2022 general election over Democrat Michael Charles Riley with seventy-seven percent (Florida Association of Counties workbook: 977 votes, 77.05%). Traylor then died over Thanksgiving weekend 2022, of a medical issue he had been battling that year (The Wewa News, December 2022). That death — not any move by subject — is what left District 2 vacant until Governor DeSantis appointed Jack Husband on July 28, 2023 (The Wewa News, September 2023, states the transition "was necessitated by the unfortunate passing of Mr. Billy Traylor").

CURRENT STATUS — THE SEAT TODAY

The District 2 seat is held by Jack Husband (Republican). The county's own District 2 page states he was appointed by Governor DeSantis on July 28, 2023, and elected to a full term in twenty twenty-four. The Supervisor of Elections' county officials list carries him as Commissioner, District 2, with a retention election in twenty twenty-six.

Subject himself remains in county public service in a different uniform: the Gulf County fire department contact list (dated February twenty twenty-six) names Ward McDaniel as Fire Chief of the Wetappo Creek Fire Department, Wewahitchka.

FILE NOTE — UNRESOLVED ITEMS (COULD NOT VERIFY)

  • RESOLVED 2026-11-09: Why the District 2 seat stood vacant for Governor DeSantis to fill in July twenty twenty-three — see the 2022 addendum in the Service Record above. Subject lost the 2022 Republican primary to Billy Traylor; Traylor won the general election and died over Thanksgiving weekend 2022, leaving the seat vacant until the July 28, 2023 appointment.
  • An April twenty twenty-six U.S. Army Corps of Engineers permit letter for the Cape San Blas beach project still carries subject's name as the District 2 owner contact. Against the county's own current pages this reads as a stale template, not a return to office. Recorded here so no future reader mistakes it for one.

ANALYST'S ASSESSMENT

A long-service local official, not a statehouse name: three terms' worth of minutes, repeated turns in the chairman's seat, hurricane emergency sessions, and a formal send-off from his own board. The "Gulf seat" of the twenty ten headline is this District 2 commission seat — won at the ballot box, held for roughly twelve years, and lost at the ballot box in twenty twenty-two to Billy Traylor — the incumbent displaced in the twenty ten cycle that brought subject in; Traylor's death weeks after his win is what a gubernatorial appointment filled in twenty twenty-three. The subject's post-office trail does not go dark; it goes to the fire station. As a file, he is exactly what open sources are good at: a completely documented public career, assembled without touching anything private.
